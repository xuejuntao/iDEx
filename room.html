The method

The device uses a low-cost reagent film layered over a disposable microfluidic strip. Target contaminants (lead, nitrates, and selected bacteria) produce a colorimetric shift that a phone camera reads under controlled lighting from a snap-on housing. Calibration is handled in software, so the hardware stays inexpensive and manufacturable.

Bench testing across 120 samples showed agreement with lab reference methods within acceptable tolerance for a screening-grade device. The remaining work is ruggedizing the housing, sourcing the reagent film at volume, and designing for manufacture — which is where a hardware co-founder comes in.

Market signal

At-home water-quality testing demand is rising with aging infrastructure and lead-exposure concern; existing consumer kits are either cheap-but-unreliable strips or accurate-but-costly lab mail-ins — a gap this idea targets. ⌁ source: iDEx market index · consumer water safety · 2026

Similar / prior work

Several microfluidic colorimetric assays appear in academic literature; most are lab-bound and not optimized for low-cost consumer manufacture. No dominant consumer product combines phone-read colorimetry with a disposable low-cost strip at this price point. ⌁ source: prior-art scan · 42 references reviewed · patents + publications

Feasibility pointers

  • ✓ Core chemistry is validated at bench scale (120 samples).
  • ✓ Phone-read calibration shifts cost from hardware to software — favorable.
  • ⚠ Reagent-film shelf life and supply at volume is the key open risk.
